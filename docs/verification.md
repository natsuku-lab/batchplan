# Verification

What the test suite pins down, so you know what to rely on. Every new test was first shown to fail with the bug it guards against planted in the code ("planting ... fails a test" below).

`BATCHPLAN_REQUIRE_GPU=1 cargo test --release` runs every test, including the code in these guides, which compiles as doctests. `--features lerobot` adds the export tests and the README's and training-data guide's code, `--features usd` the OpenUSD tests, and `--no-default-features` runs the CPU-only build's. All configurations pass on a Ryzen AI Max+ 395 with a Radeon 8060S (Vulkan) and on an M4 Pro (Metal); CI runs them on Linux with the kernels on Mesa's llvmpipe. An earlier version of the suite (14 tests) also passed on an NVIDIA T4 (Vulkan).
- **FK:** URDF forward kinematics matches Franka's published DH parameters to 1e-5.
- **Collision gradients:** analytic gradients match finite differences.
- **Trajectory optimization:**
  - The first L-BFGS step has no history, so it is steepest descent. Its direction, scaled to its largest entry, matches finite differences of the smoothness cost and of the full cost (smoothness plus collision sampled along the spline) on every device.
  - On reaches whose straight line a ball blocks, GPU and CPU step directions agree to 5e-5 at the first step and to 4e-4 at the fourth, which comes from the two-loop recursion over three remembered steps. Both devices lower the full trajectory cost alike: from 13,582 to 390 over 108 paths on the M4 Pro, and from 10,704 to 307 over 81 on the Radeon.
  - The two-loop recursion equals an explicitly built BFGS inverse Hessian, and a line search that finds nothing cheaper resets the history.
  - Planting a swapped weight in the GPU parameter packing, steepest ascent, an ignored step size, a reversed recursion loop, a stuck history ring or a wrong scaling fails a test.
  - With no steps taken, a problem's first two seeds are its searched spline and the straight line to its goal. A problem searched to its straight line is not optimized, and a budget spent while searching starts no optimization. Plans without seeds do not depend on the optimization's weights, since repairs keep their own. Planting the earlier seeding (random bends after the searched spline), optimization of every problem, a budget left to the optimizer's rounds, or repairs that take the options' weights fails a test.
- **GPU vs CPU, 20,000 configurations:** clearances agree to 3e-7 m, and gradients to 3e-4 relative.
- **GPU vs CPU IK:** the two agree on all 2,048 seeds.
- **GPU plans under an independent check:** every GPU plan reported valid is re-checked on the CPU at 4× denser interpolation. None penetrates; the worst clearance is +0.1 mm.
- **Every device behaves the same:**
  - Malformed batches (wrong array lengths, a world index past the end, worlds uploaded to another device) are an `Err` on both CPU and GPU.
  - Obstacle-free worlds plan on both.
  - Plans keep the worlds of their problems when one world holds several targets.
  - A problem's IK solves bit for bit alike alone and in a batch (on a GPU, on the same side of its lane switch), and its plan, optimization seeds included, alike anywhere in its batch; a pick's motions by a grasp are the same alone as among other grasps. Problems that differ start IK from random seeds of their own, and a problem given twice from the same ones. Planting the generator that the batch's problems shared, each drawing after those before it, fails each test, and planting the same draws for every problem fails the IK test.
- **Allowed contacts:**
  - On every device, a bar through the open fingers collides until the world lets the hand touch it. Letting one finger touch it leaves the other colliding, and links the world does not name still collide with the same obstacle and with others.
  - IK solves a grasp inside an object, and a plan reaches it, only with the touch. `MeshModel` skips the same pairs. A held box rests on a table only on the holder's device, where the world may name it.
  - Unknown links and obstacle indices are input errors on every device and in `MeshModel`.
  - The SIMD lanes match the scalar twin on worlds with touches. The GPU matches the CPU holding 60 balls, so that sets span two 64-bit words on the CPU and three 32-bit words on the GPU, with a distance grid beside the sets in the GPU's world data and touched itself.
  - Worlds uploaded for the robot without the balls give the robot holding them the same clearances as worlds uploaded for it, the balls touching nothing; a robot holding something else at those links is refused them.
  - Removing an obstacle takes its touches with it and renumbers the rest, so pick-and-place from worlds that let the hand touch the box makes the same episodes.
  - Planting a missing skip in the scalar twin, the lanes' `keeps` or `clearance`, the GPU kernels or `MeshModel`, a GPU that reads only a set's first word, a missing bound on a set's words on either device, a wrong row stride, grids that do not move past the touch sets, or no check of the uploading robot's links fails a test.
- **Why requests fail:**
  - `Device::contacts` lists what 2,000 random Panda configurations come within 5 cm of, on every device: its nearest world and self contacts equal `evaluate`'s exact clearances, nearest first, and both devices list the same. A table the world lets every link touch, and a table under the base, which no joint moves, name no contact, and a configuration that is not finite is an input error, to `evaluate` as to `contacts`, where it would read as clear of everything. Planting contacts that ignore touches, check links no joint moves or take a NaN, or an `evaluate` that takes one, fails a test.
  - On every device, a plan whose start is boxed around the hand fails as `StartCollides`, and one whose goal is, as `GoalCollides`, each naming `panda_hand` in the box; a free reach has no failure. A search that may not sample fails as `NoPath` and nothing else, no time at all as `OutOfTime`, and a slowed Panda moving too fast toward a ball to brake as `NoSpline`. Planting the ends swapped, a deadline that leaves no trace, or a found path forgotten fails the tests.
  - `ik::failures` calls a pose 2 m away `Unreachable` and a pose boxed around the hand `Collides`, naming the hand. Among 200 targets with balls in the hand's way, more than five are reached only once the push away from obstacles is off; planting no second solve calls them out of reach.
  - A stopped straight move names what its next step collides with: the slab under the hand, and nothing for a pose out of reach. Planting only the first of the group the check answers in leaves it empty. Every stopped task candidate names what it collides with (the slab over the grasp, the block over the approach, the box around the start), and a place that may not touch the table names the held object on it; planting the straight moves' or the held object's contacts dropped fails the test.
- **Tool paths:**
  - On every device, a 10 cm straight move stays within 1 mm and 0.01 rad of the line, and ends at its pose. A turn in place keeps the hand where it is. An L-shaped path keeps its precision through the corner at 5 mm steps and at 5 cm ones.
  - A slab across the way stops the motion between a tenth and three fifths of the way down, as a `Collision`, and what it returns keeps clear of the slab. A pose out of reach stops it as `Unreachable` after a straight stretch. A move across the UR5e's wrist singularity stops short of it with 300 IK iterations and passes it with 1,000, the wrist swinging over 2.5 rad, the hand on the line throughout.
  - From 400 starts near the Panda's joint limits, every motion keeps the limits along its whole spline. In 200 random pours (up to a radian of turn over half a millimetre to six), the motions stop on the way in at most one in fifty and keep the precision. A start past the limits is an input error.
  - An approach that ends around a bar succeeds only where the hand may touch it. CPU and GPU devices return the same motions, and malformed paths and options are input errors.
  - Planting a missing midpoint check, a missing check of the spline against the path, an end the motion does not rest at, ignored collisions of the spline, unbounded halving, an unchecked joint limit (21 of 221 such motions left their limits), a sample placed by position alone, or an unchecked start fails a test.

  - A picked box is set down at the second of two poses, the first taken by another box: the hand lowers it along `lower`, lets go at the grasp and backs off along its z axis; without the table as its support it cannot be let go of there.   - Grasps of a box, ball, cylinder and capsule lie `depth` inside the surface, face into it and close on no more than the opening; a distance grid has none. `place_poses` turns a box onto itself 2 or 4 ways and a round object `turns`. Malformed tasks and options, starts past the limits, rotations that are not unit and a place on a device that already holds the object are input errors.
  - Grasps of a 2.5 cm cube with a floor and fingertips 12 mm past the frame: the four from above hold it as deep as keeps the fingertips at the floor, the sixteen from the sides at the full depth, none from below; a floor over the cube leaves none and says so, and a box wider than the opening says how wide. A Panda picks a cube narrower than twice its fingertips' reach only with grasps kept off the table; at the full depth every candidate names the table. Planting no shallower grasps from above, or grasps from below kept, fails the test.
  - Planting a palm that may touch, every IK miss called out of reach, a move in not reversed, `min_fraction` ignored or shortening a move stopped out of reach, a tool speed of NaN taken, plans left unsorted, an unchecked start or a lowering run backward fails a test, as did a lift world that keeps the object or ignores its supports, the object attached at the grasp rather than its inverse, a place that ignores its supports, or a place's hand at the grasp's inverse.
- **CPU IK and retiming:** IK solves targets taken from collision-free configurations; retiming respects velocity limits and keeps the endpoints.
- **SIMD collision checks:**
  - Lanes checking eight configurations at a time give the same world and self clearances as the scalar twin of the GPU kernels, to 2e-5 m. This holds over 3,000 random configurations against every obstacle kind, on the Panda (fingers locked or sliding), the Panda holding a box, MuJoCo Menagerie's 2F-85 (quartic mimic joints) and the UR5e. Runs of worlds cross the groups of eight.
  - Their yes-or-no check (`keeps`) of a 1 cm margin agrees with the scalar twin's exact clearances away from the boundary, at the CPU's best SIMD level and at the baseline.
  - Planting ignored link-bound gating, a flipped sine, a dropped quartic term, a dropped self buffer, an ignored prismatic offset, a cylinder's missing corner, a flipped planar rotation for upright boxes, an obstacle's surrounding sphere of zero radius or a self-collision sphere of zero reach fails the test.
- **Edge checks:**
  - On every device, 400 queries over random edges (how many edges from the first are free, or which is the first free one) get the same answers as checking every state one by one, and CPU and GPU answers are identical.
  - An edge 5–10 mm from an obstacle is free, fails to keep a 1 cm clearance, and keeps it where its ends are near the path's ends.
  - Planting a skipped pass of the rake, skipped self-collision, link bounds without their radius, an inverted answer, an ignored clearance or an ignored ramp fails a test.
- **RRT-Connect and shortcutting:**
  - Every edge RRT-Connect returns is collision-free under a CPU check four times denser, on both devices. Paths start at the start and end at a goal, and only straight or bent lines (at most four waypoints) have edges longer than the range.
  - A straight line blocked halfway by a small ball is bent around it, with at most four waypoints and within 10% of the straight length. Planting a search that skips bending fails the test.
  - With a 20 cm ball where the hand passes halfway along the straight line, 48 random Panda reaches plan paths 3.8% longer than straight on average, two of them more than 10% longer. Planting samples drawn from every joint's range from the first sample (7.3%, and nine over 10%) fails the test.
  - In MotionBenchMaker's `bookshelf_tall_panda` problem 1, no spline follows the path found near the straight line, so the problem is searched again over the joint ranges and solved. Planting no second search fails the test on an M4 Pro.
  - As a tree grows to 3,000 nodes, the k-d tree finds exactly the node and distance a scan of all nodes finds, taking the lowest node among equals. Planting pruning that never searches the far side, or a wrong node per lane, fails the test.
  - Helped by three threads, MotionBenchMaker's `dresser_neutral_goal` problem 53 (which RRT-Connect does not solve) grows the same trees, node for node and bit for bit, as it grows alone over 7,000 samples, keeping a clearance and then without it. Helpers answer more than a hundredth as many queries as it draws samples (half as many on 32 threads, a thirtieth or more with two cores for all four threads). Planting a forecast taken without comparing its edges fails the test, on two cores too. Run by the scheduler, the search grows the same trees in a batch (helped after the shorter searches end, once it has checked 2,000 samples) and alone (helped after 4,000). Planting a batch whose threads never help, or stop waiting for it once the shorter searches end, or a search alone that never summons them, fails the test. Searched again on the same helpers' state, as when a problem is searched again over the joint ranges, the search is helped from its own first samples. Planting helpers that go on claiming the first search's samples, or a second search that keeps the first one's state, fails the test.
  - Splines pass through the path's ends and every pinned waypoint, run along the path when every waypoint is pinned, and spread their control points evenly along it when none is. Planting ignored pins or an uneven spread fails the test.
  - Splines double their control points while fewer are free than the longer of a problem's two paths has waypoints, each time starting over spread along the smoothed path. Planting a limit on either path's waypoints alone, no limit, or growth that keeps its round fails the test.
  - A blended pin draws the curve in around its corner without stopping it, closer at each level; only an exact pin stops it there. Splines are checked at samples no farther apart than the edge resolution, however long a span, and at least as densely as before where spans are short. Planting a fixed count per span fails the test. After a change, a check starts no later than the first sample that changed and runs to the end. Planting a start at the changed control point's own span, rather than the first span it reaches, fails the test.
  - Across 512 pick-and-place tasks with a wall, posts, an arch or a cluster of boxes across the carry, the arm never touches the scene, and at most 20 carries stop on their way (5 on an M4 Pro, at corners no blend clears). Planting splines that keep every stop the fitting pinned (43 carries stop) fails the test.
  - Shortcutting never lengthens a path or moves its ends, and every new edge passes the same denser check.
  - On a detour over a ball, with the wrist turned 1.5 rad out and back, straightening leaves the wrist with no turn (0.84 rad without straightening) and the path clear.
  - Fixed seeds give identical paths; changing the seed changes them.
  - `plan` solves every UR5e problem RRT-Connect can reach (at least 20 of 24 far reaches), its timed plans clear the CPU check, and the CPU and the GPU plan identically.
  - Continuous joints turn the short way, the long way round only when the short way is blocked, and RRT-Connect finds the way through π.

- **Obstacle distances:** every obstacle kind returns unit gradients that match finite differences, stepping back along the gradient lands on the surface, and cylinder and capsule distances match closed forms. The GPU agrees with the CPU for each kind.
- **Distance grids:**
  - Gradients match finite differences inside the grid and beyond its box, away from cell faces; so do collision gradients through a grid.
  - The GPU agrees with the CPU to 3e-7 m (Metal and RADV), including two grids in one world, one of them shared with another world and stored after a grid with an odd number of points.
  - Mesh grids never read farther than the true distance and at most a voxel diagonal nearer. Point grids never read beyond their solid voxels. Open and non-manifold meshes are refused.
  - On every device, depth images from an 8×8 time-of-flight array and from a 640×480 camera both reproduce the table under them, follow both occlusion rules, and leave unobserved space free. A fused map keeps the table, holds a ball seen through twice and clears it the third time, leaves space never observed to the rule given, and takes no hits from readings beyond its edge. Masked out of a camera's image, the Panda's spheres read free in grids and maps alike, while a box in front of the arm stays solid. What only the arm hides is solid under `Occlusion::Occupied` and free under `OccupiedExceptRobotShadow`, in one image and in a map; what the box hides stays solid, and the table hides what is below it under both. Planting a shadow treated as hidden in images, shadowed voxels treated as never observed in maps, or a GPU map that never marks shadow fails a test.
  - The GPU builds exactly the CPU's grids from point clouds and maps (under every occlusion rule), and classifies a 640×480 image's voxels and fuses it into a map with at most 0.05% of voxels differing. The CPU's integer transform reproduces the earlier floating-point one bit for bit.
  - MJCF scene meshes collide as their convex hulls; USD ones as `physics:approximation` says. Exports write each grid once, and LeRobot's environment state describes a grid by its box.
  - Planting a wrong grid offset or half-float order in the kernel, a wrong gradient axis, a missing conservative offset, a depth builder that only marks pixel points, ignored hull semantics, a map that never clears, readings clamped onto a map's edge, or a mask that ignores the arm or drops what is in front of it fails a test.   - `at` makes no allocations. `check` refuses a NaN, running too fast, a jerk spike, a moving start, leaving a joint range and the wrong joint count. It measures a joint's range on the curve itself, span by span, so a moving start's first control point, which lies behind the start, does not count.

A straight 10 cm tool path at 5 cm/s takes between 2 and 2.3 s. Bad caps and hands are input errors. Planting no stage caps (1.5 times on average), no check of the smoothed map, uniform timing without the caps, or no rotation in the hand's velocity fails a test.   - Planting a wrong basis weight in either device's gather, a wrong jerk exponent in the timing, or an allocation in `at` fails a test.
  - A trajectory starts and ends at its first and last control points, exactly; a stop starts where it was called and ends short of the path's end. Planting an end read at the start fails the test.

Planting depths clamped at zero fails the test. With the jaws held to 2 mm (`sphere_tolerances`), at 256 spheres, they reach less far than any other link and than they did, while the rest reach farther; planting the tolerances ignored fails the test.
  - Named finger links replace the links below the palm, and a block against the SO-101's fixed jaw, part of its palm's link, is touched only with them named; planting them ignored fails the test. A link of the SO-101 that turns just above its base is the only one `World::always_touching` finds on its table, not a block it rests in by default or a slab just under the table; plans from rest fail `StartCollides` without the touch and solve with it. A turntable's table, on a continuous joint, is found in a slab level with it. Planting the default configuration alone, a looser touch, or samples of a continuous joint over an unbounded range fails the test.
  - Collision-model files round-trip, SRDF pairs stop being checked, and `package://` paths resolve.
  - The UR5e, SO-101 and Panda (with fitted spheres) each plan 12 of 12 tabletop motions on the CPU and the GPU.
  - A Panda with fitted spheres, whose base spheres reach into the table it stands on, is collision-free on it and plans on every device, while a pin through its elbow still collides. Planting the base back into either device's world check fails the test.
- **Mesh clearances:**
  - Distances to every obstacle kind match closed forms to 0.1 mm, follow the joints, stay exact among several obstacles in any order, and read zero across a surface. Self clearance between links matches too, and a concave link is measured on its surface, not its hull.
  - Against a grid of a cube, clearance stays within the grid's documented slack. Held objects are checked as part of the robot, and the Panda's meshes load and read clear of its own table.
  - Fitted spheres never read farther than the meshes over 300 random configurations.
  - Planting cylinders along the wrong axis, unturned link poses, grid points left in the link frame, dropped self pairs, vertex-only grid checks, dropped held shapes, an unsorted search, a search that stops at the hulls, or hulls in place of meshes fails a test.
- **MJCF:**
  - Menagerie's Panda matches our URDF Panda link for link to 1e-5.
  - Menagerie's UR5e matches the UR5e URDF up to constant per-link frame offsets (within 3 mm; Menagerie rounds a few dimensions), and plans like the others.
  - A handwritten model pins MuJoCo's conventions against hand-composed transforms: degrees by default, intrinsic Euler sequences, `axisangle`, `xyaxes`, `zaxis`, joint anchors, several joints per body, includes, default classes and `fromto`. Planting radians as the default, extrinsic Euler composition, ignored anchors, or static bodies in the robot fails a test.
- **OpenUSD:**
  - Handwritten fixtures pin UsdPhysics conventions against hand-composed transforms:
    - Y-up centimeters, a payload and a variant fallback;
    - flat and nested bodies, `localPos1` ≠ 0, a joint authored child to parent;
    - both mimic schemas, an instanced collider, and unauthored primitive sizes.
  - Planting a wrong up-axis rotation, ignored units, an unflipped swapped joint, an ignored `localPos1`, mimic offsets left in degrees, a wrong PhysX gearing sign, skipped instance proxies or a wrong default cube size fails a test.
  - newton-assets' UR5e matches the Menagerie MJCF it was converted from exactly. Their 2F-85 mimics its driver and fits spheres to mesh colliders read from a binary layer.
  - A binary STL whose header starts with "solid", as CAD exporters write it, loads as its ASCII twin does, with a carriage return in its triangle count; without telling binary from ASCII by the file's size, it fails to load.
  - The Franka converted from our URDF matches it to 1e-5, with identical self-collision results under the same collision model.
  - `scripts/validate_usd.py` rebuilds every fixture's kinematics with Pixar's `usd-core` and agrees with batchplan to within 6e-6.
- **Constraints:**
  - A gripper carried upright past a pillar, within 5°, keeps the bound at 1 kHz along every valid path, searched or optimized, and is collision-free, on the CPU and the GPU, where most of the same problems planned without the constraint tilt past it; optimization keeps some seeds and shortens some paths. A hand kept in a box, its tilt and its twist bounded, keeps all three, where unconstrained paths leave them. An arm carries upright while the other arm's joints keep their values bit for bit. Every timed constrained path passes `Trajectory::check`, and a spline fitted through a path that starts at a joint's limit keeps its control points within the joint's range.
  - Two arms carry a tray level over a block, the right hand pinned in the left hand's frame: every path keeps the right hand within 2 mm and 0.02 rad of its place at 1 kHz and the left within 5° of straight down, where unconstrained paths pull the hands centimeters apart. The carries average within 1.14 times the unconstrained paths' length (1.01; 0.98 to 1.13 over 24 sampling seeds); with only straight shortcuts they were 1.24 (1.17 to 1.66). A goal 1.5 mm off its place plans; one 4 mm off is an input error.
  - Optimized seeds that break their constraints are invalid: stopped after one step, the straight lines optimization starts from tilt past the bound, and every seed still valid keeps it. `Robot::breach` tests a state as `plan` tests its ends: a hand turned past a twist bound breaks it, a bound below IK's precision counts as that precision, and constraints a plan refuses are input errors; planting a test that always passes, or no check of the constraints, fails it. Splines of 10 control points too short to follow a 1° bound are repaired within it, and every reach under 4 rad plans with 10, which planting growth before a reach is searched again over the joint ranges breaks. With 7 control points, one free, every two-arm tray carry plans with more, keeping its bounds at 1 kHz and collision-free, while its optimized seeds keep 7. Planting no growth, or optimization started from a grown spline, fails the test. A problem whose spline grew is optimized even within 2% of its straight line, which planting the old skip fails.
  - Blends keep a sequence's constraints, where the same blends without them tilt the hand past its bound. A place carries the box upright over a wall it would otherwise tip it past, a pose reached tilted stops at the transit, the pose behind it gets its own transit, and a pick whose hand must stay near its start stops at the transit too. A follower's replan around a box keeps the gripper upright, where a replan without the constraint tilts it; kept near its goal, where it is not yet, it stops instead of replanning; and a trajectory whose goal breaks the follower's constraints is an input error.
  - The same constrained problems plan identically alone, in a batch with unconstrained ones, and on either device, and another sampling seed changes some of them, so they come from sampling.
  - Ends that break their constraints, a hand the robot lacks, a constraint relative to its own hand or a missing one, a negative bound and a bounded twist without a tilt bound are input errors that say what was wrong, through `plan`, `join` and the tasks; a task names itself.
  - Inside, the lanes judge constraints exactly as the scalar test does on states that keep and break each kind of bound, in the world and relative to another hand, at the best SIMD level and the baseline; each projection row changes at the rate the Jacobians of both hands give, and the optimizer's cost at the rate of its gradient. The GPU's optimization steps match the CPU's on constrained problems, upright reaches and two-arm carries: within 1e-3 of their largest entry at the first step and 1e-2 at the second on every path, and the carries within 1e-2 at the fourth, where the constraints' cost in the third line search shows. The reaches stop at the second: where a step cancels a stiff constraint's gradient, f32 differences in where the next steps land grow to about 1e-2 by the fourth, as they do between two CPU runs whose gradients differ by 1e-5. Validation refuses a path that breaks its constraints between its ends. Inside, on a hand pinned in the other's frame (`pinned_pandas`), a spline fitted through three waypoints whose edges leave the bounds keeps them at every free control point's knot, and a shortcut's walk in hops four default hops long keeps them at every hop's middle; without the fit's projection or the walk's halving each fails.
  - Planting a wrong sign or axis in the lanes, a row or the GPU's cost, a frame's part left out of a row, the projection or the gradient, the floors not doubled relative to another hand, a skipped test, held joints that project, unchecked ends, splines fitted, searches run, blends, transits or replans planned without the constraints, a transit given to the wrong candidate, optimization or repair without their cost, a GPU residual taken as the difference of two angles (on RADV), validation without their test, a follower that takes a goal outside them, held joints rounded by the spline fit, control points fitted past a joint's limit, or a search seeded by its batch fails a test.
- **Adapter errors:** adapters below the required limits are rejected with the reason, and an unknown adapter name is a clear error.
- **Embedding:**
  - A one-thread CPU device starts exactly one thread and no others while it plans; planting one use of rayon's global pool fails the test.
  - `plan`, `rrt::connect` and `shortcut::shortcut` build each search on the thread that runs it: per problem, the calling thread allocates 3, 4 and 1 times, for the results' copies of the problems. Planting searches built on the calling thread (32, 16 and 7) fails the test, at each of the three.
  - A 1 ms time budget returns within one more chunk of work than the budget, on the CPU and the GPU, and searches stop at the budget too. Without a budget, two runs give identical paths. Planting a backend or a search that ignores the deadline fails a test. These tests run one at a time in `tests/budget.rs`, since other tests' threads beside them swing wall-clock times past the slack. With rayon's global pool kept busy for a second, the GPU device plans at once, on threads of its own; planting its searches on the global pool makes it wait the second.
  - Errors have the documented kinds: a malformed batch is `Input`, an unknown adapter `Gpu`, a bad file `Load` with its path, an unsafe trajectory `Unsafe`.
  - An attached box moves with the hand, collides with an obstacle between the fingers that the bare hand misses, and collides with the fingers unless they are touch links. Collision gradients through it match finite differences, and detaching restores the robot exactly. Devices made with `with_robot` plan around the box in worlds uploaded to the original device, and see it exactly as a device made for the held robot does, on the CPU and the GPU. Attaching outside a rayon pool leaves the calling thread out of rayon. A held box is covered without fitting: every sampled point of it lies in a sphere, no sphere reaches past it by more than the tolerance, and the sphere budget holds. A held ball is exactly one sphere. Spheres 5% too small, or overhang measured from the wrong side of a cell, fail the test. Planting ignored touch links, a wrong kinematic chain, a GPU device that keeps the old robot, a detach that keeps the spheres, or fitting in a one-thread pool on the calling thread (which rayon never unregisters) fails a test.
  - Relocked joints move their links, mimic joints' links included, to where a robot loaded at the new values has them, and to the poses their mimic curves give. A device made with `with_robot` for a relocked Panda checks collisions as one made for the Panda loaded there, against worlds uploaded before, on the CPU and the GPU, and the GPU reuses the kernels it built. A joint locked when the collision model was fitted stays at its value: relocking it is refused, and so is loading the model with it free or locked elsewhere, through a model saved and loaded again and two arms assembled from it too. A NaN lock value is refused at load and on relock. Planting mimic links left in place, the curve ignored, a relock that changes nothing, kernels that read the origins, no check on relocking or on loading, a fit that records nothing, a record not saved or not assembled, or a NaN lock accepted fails a test.

## Running the tests

```bash
BATCHPLAN_REQUIRE_GPU=1 cargo test --release                       # fail instead of skipping GPU tests without a GPU
BATCHPLAN_REQUIRE_GPU=1 cargo test --release --features lerobot,usd
cargo test --release --no-default-features                         # the CPU-only build
```

Without `BATCHPLAN_REQUIRE_GPU`, tests that need a GPU skip silently when no adapter exists. `scripts/validate_lerobot.py` and `scripts/validate_usd.py` check exports and USD loading against the reference Python packages (`lerobot`, Pixar's `usd-core`) in a virtual environment. The first also loads datasets with the camera videos `scripts/replay_mujoco.py` renders.
