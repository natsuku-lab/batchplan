# Placing

A place takes where it may let go of what the hand holds as `Release`s ([picking up and setting down](planning.md#picking-up-and-setting-down)). `place_poses` gives a shape's symmetric turns at one spot; `rests_on` finds where it rests on a support cameras see, and `falls_into` where to let it go to fall into a container they see.

## Releases

A `Release` is the object's `pose` where the hand lets go, how far it `drop`s from there and a `cost`, as a hold's ([costs](planning.md#costs-and-how-many-will-do)). A plain `Pose` is a release at drop and cost 0; `Release::from(&rest)` and `Release::from(&fall)` take a rest's or a fall's.

- **Release above a measured rest.** Give each release the object's pose where the fingers open and, where it lies a known vertical distance above its rest at the same attitude, that distance as its `drop` (`falls_into` gives the drop it finds). For a positive drop, the follower checks departure against `TaskPlan::falling` in the departure world, with contact allowed only for the releasing fingers (the hand links if it has no finger links). `TaskPlan::placed` gives the expected landing pose for subsequent planning. Verify the actual placement after release. A container depth or rim height alone does not establish where an object will land.
- **How far the hand opens.** A place opens the hand to where the robot was loaded with its fingers; `Place::open` names narrower openings for a spot without room for the open fingers ([finger openings](grasps.md#finger-openings)).

## Rests on what cameras see

`rests_on(&device, &[RestProblem], &[Surface], &RestOptions)` finds where a held object can be let go on a measured support and stay: on a plate, a cabinet's top, the top of a rack. Each `RestProblem` gives the object in its own frame (a box, ball, cylinder or capsule), how it is turned as it is set down (`rotation`: upright, on its side, flat on a slope) and which surface it is set on. The surfaces are those [grasps](grasps.md#grasps-of-what-cameras-see) read.

- **The support** is the surface's `object` points (every point, where it flags none); the rest are kept `clearance` (1 cm) away, and what is under them is taken as solid too: nothing rests under a point that is not the support's, however high it is, so a shelf with another over it has rests only where the one above was not seen or is flagged with it. What is under the highest point seen is solid, a point whose normal faces down holds nothing up, and where no camera saw is empty space. Flag everything the object may lie on: a fixture measured in pieces has rests across its pieces only where all of them are flagged. `seen_from_above_holds` (on by default) lets a point whose normal faces down hold where the camera that saw it was above it, since a noisy top's normals can face down in places. It takes a face of the support that leans out over empty space, seen from above, as a top at its own height: set it to `false` for a support with such a face.
- **How it is set down.** Over each spot `spacing` (1 cm) apart across the support, at each of `turns` (8) turns of `rotation` about the vertical, the object comes straight down until it touches. It rests there if what it touches holds it, with `friction` (0.5), and still does with its weight tipped `tipping` (5°) any way: so a tall object needs its middle farther in from an edge than a flat one, and a slope holds one only that far short of `atan(friction)` unless a stop below it does. The object is not tipped or slid to where it would come to rest: set down level on a slope it touches along an edge and has no rest, so give the rotation that lies flat on the slope.
- **What rolls.** A can or a capsule laid on its side that is not held where it is set down rolls a little way across its axis, to where it lies lowest, and rests there if that holds it. So it is found in the cradle between two bars, in a rack's groove or against a lip at the foot of a slope from spots a centimeter apart, each place once a turn. A can stood on its end is not rolled, however it leans. On a flat support it rolls and has no rest, however noisily the support was read, as a ball has none; a ball rests on a rim or on posts that hold it from every side, where a spot is over them. Sides shallower than `touching` tells from flat are no cradle.
- **What it gives up.** Give the object its true shape: a round bottle given as a box has rests on a ridge the bottle would roll off. The support under a patch of contacts is taken as a plane, so a flat bottom over the top of one round bar, within `touching` of it, is read as standing on a band of the bar: where a spot lands there, as spots 2 mm apart do, a tray is found balanced over one bar, which a round bar does not hold.
- **Looking closer** (`look_closer`, on by default; `false` tries the spots alone). A rest narrower than the spots are apart, as an upright bottle's against the lip at the foot of a sloping rack, which stands in a strip a few millimeters deep, lies within a spacing of a spot where the object nearly rests: held there as set down, but tipping too easily. With `look_closer`, of those spots at a turn, the one nearest `near`, or without it the one nearest the middle of the support, has the spots a cell (`touching`) apart within `spacing` of it tried too: with `near` at every turn, and without it only where no spot rests at any turn, so a search that finds rests without `near` costs what it does without the option. One place is looked at, not every place: such a strip is found near that spot and not along its length, so a caller that wants it elsewhere points there.
- **Where you would have it** (`RestProblem::near`). A rest can be narrower than the spots are apart with nothing beside it that nearly holds: a bowl given as a flat cylinder wider than a dished plate's floor stands evenly on the plate's raised ring only within a few millimeters of the middle, so spots a centimeter apart find or miss it by where the plate was measured. `near` is a point you would have the object set down at, as the middle of the plate or where an agent pointed: within `spacing` of it, seen from above, and over the support, the object is also set down a cell of the support apart (`touching`, 2 mm), 77 more spots a turn at the defaults (more than a million is an input error), and with `look_closer` it says which place where the object nearly rests is looked at closer. Leave it out for a support with no place in mind, as a whole table, where the spots find every place already. It judges no differently: near a point on a plate's rim it finds the rests a flat cylinder has there, where a bowl on a small foot would not stay, so give the object the shape it stands on.
- **What comes back.** Per problem, each `Rest`: the object's `pose` in the surface's frame, the `support` it touches (the corners of its hull seen from above) and how far the weight can be `tipping` before the contacts let go. Pass every one to `Place::releases` (`Release::from(&rest)`), which checks the hand and the way there. A rest comes at each of the `turns`: where the shape is set down the same at two of them, as a box at a half turn, an upright can at any, or a bottle laid down end for end, the two rest in the same places and are two poses for the hand. `place_poses` adds the copies about a lying object's own axis, where a grasp needs them. They come every spot at the first turn, then the next, and within a turn the steadiest first, then spread over the support; with a `near`, nearest it first instead, in x and y, each costing its distance (`Rest::cost`), which a place's release takes.
- **Cost.** The problems of a call run in parallel on the device's threads, each surface read once however many problems name it, and a problem's rests are the same alone, among others and on either device ([numbers](benchmarks.md#rests-on-a-surface)).

## Why no rest

`rest_failures(&device, &problems, &surfaces, &rests, &o)` says, for each problem `rests_on` found no rest for, how near the search came (`RestFailure`), the furthest stage any spot and turn reached:

| `RestFailure` | How near it came |
|---|---|
| `Blocked` | a rest but for a point in its way (the steadiest such) |
| `Tips` | held but tipping by less than `tipping` (the steadiest, with how far it can tip) |
| `Unheld` | touching the support and not held (the spot nearest `near`, or the support's middle, with the unit mean of the directions the support pushes it along: up on a level support, leaning on a slope) |
| `Unsupported` | nothing of the support under it anywhere |

Where it is not held or tips, what would be in its way there even if it held comes too (`blocked`): a `Blocking` point that is not the support's says the flags leave out part of what the object would lie against; one that is says a part of the support seen only from below. Pass it the options the rests were found with: it searches the problems without rests again as `rests_on` did with them, looking closer or not and reading the support as they say, so it costs one to two more searches of those problems, and runs only when asked:

```rust,no_run
# use batchplan::{Device, Error, RestFailure, RestOptions, RestProblem, Surface};
# fn run(device: &Device, problems: &[RestProblem], surfaces: &[Surface]) -> Result<(), Error> {
let o = RestOptions::default();
let rests = batchplan::rests_on(device, problems, surfaces, &o)?;
let why = batchplan::rest_failures(device, problems, surfaces, &rests, &o)?;
for (k, failure) in why.iter().enumerate() {
    match failure {
        None => {}
        Some(RestFailure::Unheld { blocked: Some(by), .. }) if !by.support => {
            println!("problem {k}: flag what it would lie against, as {}", by.point);
        }
        Some(RestFailure::Tips { tipping, .. }) => println!("problem {k}: tips at {tipping} rad"),
        Some(other) => println!("problem {k}: {other:?}"),
    }
}
# Ok(())
# }
```

## Letting go into a container

A can into a basket, a bowl into an open drawer: the object does not rest where it is let go, it falls and comes to lie somewhere inside, and what matters is that it cannot leave. `falls_into(&device, &[RestProblem], &[Surface], &RestOptions)` takes the same problems, surfaces and options as `rests_on` and gives, per problem, each `Fall`: a `pose` to let go at, with the object's lowest point at least `clearance` over the hollow's rim and the object that far clear of all around it, how far it drops from there to where it lands as it is (`drop`), and how deep under the rim it lands (`sunk`). `Release::from(&fall)` gives a place the pose and the drop, and `Place::lower` as for any place: the move in ends at that pose, above the rim. Falls read only a problem's `object`, `surface`, `rotation` and `near`, and of the options `turns`, `spacing`, `touching`, `clearance`, `look_closer` and `seen_from_above_holds`.

- **What holds it in.** Over each spot, at each turn, the object lands on the support or on anything else there, as what is already in the basket. It is held in where the rim is higher over where it lands than its own middle is over its bottom. It lands as it is: the clearance is kept where it is let go, not where it lands, so a box that goes through a bowl's opening, and grown by the clearance would not, falls in. It keeps its turn as it moves: one that could turn through a gap it cannot pass as it is let go is taken as held.
- **Gaps.** Whether it can leave is asked of the object worn down by a level disc of half the diagonal between spots (7 mm at the defaults), so no way out between spots is missed: a gap it fits through holds nothing in, and a gap up to that much narrower than it may be taken as one it passes too. Worn down so, an object must keep at least a cell of the support (`touching`) of itself every way, or it could slip between the points seen of a solid wall: one thinner, as a ball under about 1.6 cm across or a standing pencil, has no falls; space the spots nearer for it.
- **What it needs.** A floor, or a wall sloping in under the object, seen as deep as its middle is over its bottom somewhere in the hollow: a floor no camera saw is no floor, and an upright wall is read as its top. Where some of the floor was seen that deep, the object is let go over the patches no camera saw too. A tray, a plate or a table holds nothing in, and gives nothing.
- **Looking closer** (`look_closer`, on by default; `false` tries the spots alone). A container the object barely fits holds it in only nearer one place than the spots lie apart: a box in a bowl a little wider than its diagonal lands as deep under the rim as its middle is over its bottom only within millimeters of the bowl's axis, and spots a centimeter apart may all lie 7 mm from it. With `look_closer`, the spots a cell apart within `spacing` of `near` are tried at every turn, or without it those around the spot of a hollow where the object lands the deepest under the rim, only where no spot holds it in at any turn. Falls found there come after the spots' of their turn.
- **Into or onto.** A basket has rests on its rim and falls inside it; which is wanted is the task's, as the target is, so call the one the task asks for. Falls come at every turn, those with the most room around them first and of those the nearest the middle of where it is held in, or nearest the problem's `near` first, each costing its distance, as rests do.

## The place nearest a spot

Where several picks would each leave the object in the hand and it should go as near one spot as it can, give one `place` call one `Place` per pick (its plan's `end` and `held`), each with the same releases nearest the spot first, each costing its distance in x and y from it, and `enough: Some(1)`: `rests_on` and `falls_into` give them so for a problem with `near`. Each task's first plan is then its nearest release that gets through, and the least `TaskPlan::cost` of the tasks' first plans is the hold that sets the object down nearest the spot. MoveIt Task Constructor likewise ranks a whole pick and place's solutions across its grasps, by the costs its stages give them.
