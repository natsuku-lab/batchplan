# Grasps

A pick takes its grasps as `Hold`s, each a pose of the hand in the picked object's frame ([picking up and setting down](planning.md#picking-up-and-setting-down)). `grasps` proposes them for a box, ball, cylinder or capsule; `grasps_on` finds them for any shape from what cameras see of it. Both take the hand as a `Gripper`.

## The gripper

`robot.gripper(hand, &GripperCalibration::new(loaded_gap, closed_gap, closed))?` builds a hand's `Gripper` once, from what was measured of it: how far apart the pads stand as loaded and with the fingers closed, and the finger joints' values commanded closed. Set `capacity` on the calibration where the hand grasps less than it opens (`None`: the loaded gap), and `friction` where the pads' differs from 0.5. Every contact pair a grasp closes on fits within the capacity. Build it from the robot the device runs, after any `attach` or `relock`: its grasps are checked with the hand's spheres as they were then. Pass the same gripper to `grasps`, `grasps_on`, `Grasp::closes_on`, `SurfaceGraspOptions::order_for_turn` and, in `batchplan-miru`, `Observed::grip_worlds`.

- **Openings.** `gripper.locked_at(opening)` gives the finger joints' values at which the pads stand `opening` apart, linear between the closed and the loaded joints; `gripper.opening_of(&locked)` goes the other way. `gripper.openings()` lists those a grasp with `narrow` may come in at, widest first.
- **Jaws that slide.** `narrow` takes the finger spheres to translate equally and oppositely along the closing line; `gripper.sliding()` says whether the model's do. Where they do not, `narrow` is refused: check the open hand alone (`narrow: false`).

## Grasps of what cameras see

`grasps_on(&device, &gripper, &[Surface], &SurfaceGraspOptions)` proposes grasps for any shape from points on its surfaces and the normals out of them, as cameras see it. A grasp closes on two patches of surface (each the mean of the points within `contact`, 5 mm) that face away from each other, no farther apart than the gripper's capacity, with the line between them within `atan(friction)` of each patch's normal (the pads' `friction`, 0.5: 27°): the condition under which two fingers that press with friction hold, which Dex-Net tests on meshes. A bowl's wall, a handle and a box's sides are found alike, with no shape assumed.

- **Surfaces.** `Surface::from_depth(&device, &image, &object, robot_mask, radius)` reads one depth image, on the device's threads: a point for every reading off the robot, `object` saying per pixel which show the object to grasp (a segmentation; empty for all), each of those with the normal of the plane through the readings within `radius` of it (5 mm suits), toward the camera, or none where they lie on one line or near no plane. `surface.join(other)` adds another view, and `Surface::from_points(&points, seen_from, radius)` takes a cloud seen from one place. Each point records where it was seen from (`seen_from`, the camera's position; infinite where a joined surface did not say). A `Surface` is plain data (`points`, `normals`, `object`, `seen_from`), so points with normals from elsewhere, as sampled from a mesh, go in directly, with `seen_from` empty where no camera saw them. Points that are not the object's stay in: nothing else may lie between a grasp's patches, and the hand is kept clear of them.
- **Both sides seen.** Without a shell (`shell: None`), both patches must have been seen. A surface with nothing seen behind it then gives no grasp, since where its other side lies is a guess: one camera's view of a box on a table gives none, and two views give those across what the cameras see between them. Patches nearer than 2.5 mm are one surface's noise.
- **The hand** is the robot's own, with its fingers as the locked joints hold them. Its collision spheres at the grasp, each swept back `approach` (10 cm, a pick's) along the hand's z axis, lie clear of every point: the way in is checked because a sheet of points can pass between two spheres of a finger. Only the fingers may be in what they close on, the object's points between the two patches. What no camera saw, the check cannot know: behind a wall seen from one side a finger is checked against the points seen alone, so it is known clear only where the rays went past, not in the shadow under a flared rim. A hand that is not clear is drawn back while `overlap` of the fingers stays on the patches, and `floor` holds the frame and the open fingertips above a height. To come in with the fingers part open, as past a neighboring wall, set [`narrow`](#fingers-that-come-in-narrower), or pass `device.with_robot(&relocked)?` and a gripper of `relocked = robot.relock(&fingers)?` loaded at that opening, for one opening of your choosing.
- **Cost.** The surfaces of a call run in parallel on the device's threads, and a surface's grasps are the same alone, among others and on either device ([Benchmarks](benchmarks.md#grasps-of-a-surface)).

## Walls seen from one side

A bowl seen only from above shows its rim and its inner wall; its outer wall flares out of sight under the rim. `shell` (on by default: `Some(f32::INFINITY)`, a wall as thick as the gripper grasps) grasps such a wall on evidence rather than a guess: a ray that reached something farther crossed empty space, and no ray goes into a solid. From a patch whose other side no camera saw, the second contact goes where the cameras saw past the wall, within `shell` and never past the gripper's capacity. So `width()` is the most the wall can be, not what it is, and the `Grasp` says so (`one_sided`): judge whether such a grasp holds by something other than its width. `SurfaceGraspOptions { shell: Some(0.015), .. }` takes the wall no thicker than 1.5 cm, and `shell: None` leaves it ungrasped.

- **A leaning wall**, as a flared bowl's, is closed on level too if the level line through the patch is inside the patch's friction cone (the gripper's `friction`: a wall leaning 27° needs 0.51, and rubber pads at 2 have it): a hand closing along the normal leans as the wall does and puts a finger down beside it, where one closing level comes straight down across the rim.
- **A solid** is closed on only across all of it: a box seen from one side is grasped where the cameras saw past its far side.
- **How thick.** Behind a flared wall the space the rays went past starts beyond the wall's other side: a bowl whose wall is 3 mm thick, seen from 40 cm above, is grasped 5.5 to 11.5 mm wide at its rim ([Benchmarks](benchmarks.md#grasps-of-a-surface)), so 1.5 cm suits it and 4 mm finds nothing.
- **The evidence** is each point's `seen_from`: the points seen from exactly one place make one view, of the nearest reading in each direction, so looks joined from one place are one view, and a cloud from a moving sensor, each point seen from a place of its own, shows nothing. `from_points` takes its `seen_from` as one camera's place, the space between it and every point empty: give a cloud fused from several views `shell: None`.
- **Their order.** Grasps of two seen patches come first, within the ordinary grasps and again within those [`close_on`](#closing-on-a-point) adds.

## Fingers that come in narrower

Open fingers beside a drawer's wall or a neighbor are in it, though the grasp needs a fraction of the opening. `SurfaceGraspOptions { narrow: true, .. }` (off by default) takes each finger to slide along the hand's y axis, as a parallel gripper's do, checks every grasp at the gripper's openings (`Gripper::openings`), from open down to the fingers 2.5 mm, a surface's noise, off the patches (nearer, a finger may be on its patch's wall before it closes, and push what it was to grasp), and gives each `Grasp` the `opening` it comes in with: the one that keeps the fingers farthest from what is not the object's, the floor included. A planner lets the fingers touch what they grasp and nothing else, and knows what stands around less finely than the points do (a distance grid errs toward collision), so room on the object's side buys nothing there. A finger inside a bowl stands midway between its walls, and a box with nothing near is grasped open, as without the option.

A grasp the open hand has stays where it is, drawn back as the open hand is, so `narrow` moves no grasp found without it and adds those the open hand has not: there the hand comes in as deep as it is clear narrower. The floor is kept at each opening, the lower finger standing higher as they near. Lock the fingers there for the pick ([finger openings](#finger-openings)): `Hold::new(pose, grasp.opening, &gripper)` holds each grasp with the finger joints at `gripper.locked_at(grasp.opening)` (the Panda's two fingers each stand at half of it; as loaded at the loaded gap), and a call's grasps share at most sixteen openings. A grasp that is refused costs about what the open hand's check does, and one that is found is checked across each finger's whole slide: 30 percent more for the Panda's hand of 22 spheres on a bowl beside a wall, and more for a hand of many ([Benchmarks](benchmarks.md#grasps-of-a-surface)).

## Room a planner needs

`clearance` (0 by default) keeps every sphere of the hand that far from what it does not close on, its way in included: the hand is drawn back to where it has that room, while `overlap` of its fingers stays on the patches, or its grasp is left out. Without it the hand is checked against the points with nothing to spare, and a planner's distance grids err toward collision by more than the points do: a hand the points find clear, a millimeter from a drawer's front behind the handle it grips, is refused at its reach. `SurfaceGraspOptions { clearance: 0.0025, .. }` suits worlds of distance grids a few millimeters fine. A part that stands little proud of what it is fixed to leaves less than the default `overlap` with room behind it: give it the patch's radius (`contact`). MoveIt Grasps pads the hand on its approach likewise.

## Closing on a point

`close_on` is a point in the surface's frame the fingers must close at, such as a reading on one bar among several. The ordinary grasps keep their order and come first, those that miss the point included. After them come approaches at finer angles about the contact pairs near the point, those whose fingers close on it, two seen sides first. Last come shallower insertions of every grasp that encloses the point, in their parent grasps' order: the hand drawn back while `overlap` of the fingers stays on the patches, each checked again and still enclosing the point. These can clear the palm where the deepest insertion collides. A failed search can visit more candidates. `near` orders the ordinary grasps independently.

`grasp.closes_on(&gripper, at, radius)` checks that a whole ball fits between the opposing pads at the grasp's opening and clears the hand: pass the gripper `grasps_on` used, and apply it to the ordinary grasps too when choosing a motion. The optional crate `batchplan-miru` projects a candidate into its own world and keeps the remaining obstacles after a successful lift ([its grips page](../miru/docs/grips.md)).

## What comes back

Per surface, each pair of patches from `turns` (8) directions around its line, each both ways round, as a `Grasp`: the hand's `pose`, the two `contacts` (`width()` apart) and the `opening` the fingers come in with, in the surface's frame. The order suits trying the first few: every pair from above (where its line is not upright), then every pair a turn to either side, and so on round; within a turn, the pairs within half a friction cone of square before the rest, each the farthest from those before it, so they spread over the object.

- **First touch** (`first_touch`, on by default). Within each turn, of the grasps of two seen sides and again of those of one, the grasps whose fingers close onto their patches before they meet any other part of what was seen come first, so from above still comes before a turn to either side. A finger closing level deep across a wall that leans over its patch meets the wall above the patch, a finger's length from the other contact, and the object turns in the hand as it is lifted, so those grasps follow; none is left out. It sweeps each finger of each grasp over the object's patches ([Benchmarks](benchmarks.md#grasps-of-a-surface)); `first_touch: false` saves that and keeps each turn's grasps in the order they came.
- **Near a point** (`near`). Within each of those tiers, the grasps whose patches' middle lies nearest the point come first: for a caller told where to grip, as on a knob's axis or at a handle's middle. It reorders the grasps and finds no others.

## Using them in a pick

`Pick::grasps` are in the picked obstacle's frame: bring each pose there, `obstacle.pose().inverse().mul_pose(g.pose)`, and hold it at its opening, `Hold::new(pose, grasp.opening, &gripper)` (as loaded at the loaded gap). When any grip on the object will do and the next motion depends on where it sits in the hand, give each hold `cost: grasp.unseen_span()`: two observed contacts come first, then the tighter one-sided width bounds. This measures where the unseen wall could end; friction, compliance and slip still affect where the object seats. For a grip asked for near a point, keep the cost 0 and the grasps' order. Pass every grasp in its order and set `Pick::enough` ([how many will do](planning.md#costs-and-how-many-will-do)).

## Finger openings

- **Where the fingers are.** A robot loaded with its fingers locked models them where it locked them. A pick's grasps may each keep them narrower (`Hold::locked`, per grasp the fingers' joint values, as `Robot::relock` takes them; empty as loaded), as for a rim between walls that the open fingers would hit: one pick takes grasps at several openings and plans by any of them, a relocked robot for each opening, which builds nothing. Each plan's `held` starts with its grasp's values, and its transit and move in are run with the fingers there: follow them on that robot too (`device.with_robot(&robot.relock(&held.locked)?)?` for a `Follower`'s ticks), since the device for the robot as loaded checks the open fingers and stops where they are in what the narrow ones pass. A hold (`Held::locked`) says where the fingers closed, and every call that holds the object (`place`, `plan`, `follow`, `join`, IK, `handover`) checks the robot so. The collision model must be fitted with the fingers free, as `relock` requires.
- **How far the hand opens.** A place opens the hand to where the robot was loaded with its fingers, and solves where it lets go with them there, so a spot without room for the open fingers is refused at its release. `Place::open` names the values the fingers may open to instead, as `Held::locked` names them, in the order preferred (widest first, say: `Gripper::openings()` gives those a grasp with `narrow` may come in at, and `Gripper::locked_at` the fingers' values at each). Where the hand lets go is then solved with the fingers where they hold the object, and each pose lets go at the first opening with which the hand is clear there and on its move away, the object standing where it was set and the fingers free to touch it; the plan says which (`TaskPlan::opening`). Command that opening, and follow the move away on the robot relocked at it. The openings share the place's IK, its move in and its transit: each one tried costs a move away. Joints an opening does not name are as loaded, whatever the hold has them at. The next opening is tried only where the fingers themselves stopped the one before (out of reach, or the arm or palm in something, is the same at each), and a pose none clears stops at its departure, with what the last one tried met. After a grasp that missed, likewise: open only as far as the fingers came in, and plan or follow the way back on the robot relocked there.
